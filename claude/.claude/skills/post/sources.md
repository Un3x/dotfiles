# Sources

Read from step 2 of `post`. Thomas criticises studies in public (« et si je lisais l'article ? »): his own post cannot be caught with the same flaw.

1. **Get the primary source**: the study's PDF or the advisory, not the article about it. A PDF comes back binary through WebFetch: download it to the scratchpad and run `pdftotext -layout`. A news item without a primary source is said to be secondary in the hand-over.
2. **Read it with the grid**:
   - Measured or declared? How are the numbers computed (Undo: hours derived from an assumed 40 h week).
   - Mean, median or buckets? What the mean hides (a two-speed population).
   - Is there a before? Without one, no change can be claimed.
   - The panel: size, country, sector, company size, date of the data (LocalStack: 309 people, US and Canada, companies over 1,000 staff).
   - Who pays, and what they sell.
   - Correlation or causation, and in which direction.
   - Does the source say what the post will say?
3. **Write down** for the hand-over: each figure the post uses, with its exact wording in the source, and the limit a commenter would raise.
